Most modern Android devices do not need calibration because of the accuracy of the quartz crystal oscillators these devices contain.  But if you suspect that your device is inaccurate in measuring absolute pitch, TuneLab provides a means to calibrate that device.  Calibrating to an unreliable source can be worse than doing no calibration at all, so if you are going to do a calibration, make sure it is to a reliable source.

If you decide to do a calibration, you only need to do it once.  Thereafter TuneLab will remember the results of that calibration and use it to adjust for any inaccuracy in the sound system of your device.  Calibration is not for tuning to a non-standard pitch, such as A-442.  Use the offset on the main tuning page for that instead.

The preferred method of calibration is Internet Calibration. If you select Internet Calibration, you must leave your device running the calibration routine which by default takes 6 hours.  This is best done overnight and with your device plugged in to a charger when you are unlikely to need it for anything else.  If your Internet connection is fast enough, depending on the desired accuracy, you might be able to complete the calibration in  as little as 20 minutes.  After about 2 minutes a button will appear showing you how accurate the calibration would be if it were ended now.  Follow the prompts on the screen after selecting this option. If this process is interrupted for any reason before completing normally, the calibration will be aborted, and you will have to start it over from the beginning. Internet Calibration accesses Internet time-servers only very briefly at the beginning and again at the end of the calibration period. See the manual for more details on how Internet Calibration works.

For a faster calibration you will need a tone with a trusted pitch to use as the reference.  Do not trust a tuning fork to provide this reference.  Use a good reliable electronically-generated tone. On this page you select the pitch you will be using. Three common pitch sources are offered as choices. If you want to use some other frequency, there is a choice that lets you enter any frequency explicitly. Do not use a tone from a website or from an uncalibrated program. Your device's default calibration is already better than that even before you do a custom calibration.

The NIST tones referred to in the first choice are the tones available from the National Institute of Standards and Technology in Colorado, USA. The tones are available by telephone by calling NIST at (303)499-7111. Do not use your Android phone to make this call. Use a separate phone with a loud enough speaker to be heard by TuneLab.
