Audio Triggering is used to control several operations within TuneLab. A Trigger is detected when the sound detected at the microphone rises suddenly. The purpose of detecting a Trigger is to detect the start of a note being played.  This detection is obviously important in measuring inharmonicity and pre-measuring for a pitch-raise in over-pull mode.  But it is also used in restarting the grouping of audio samples for the Spectrum Display, for restarting an Offset Locking operation, and optionally for auto note switching.  On the auto note switching setup page you can choose to make auto note switching work only on triggers at the beginning of a note being played rather than all the time.  This will reduce the chances that auto note switching will switch to the wrong note because the note is decaying and subject to more noise.  Because of the many uses of Triggering, the volume indicator bar on the right side of the Spectrum Display shows when a trigger is detected by a slight change in color.  Whenever a trigger is detected the color alternates between a blue tint and a lavender tint to verify that triggers are being detected correctly.

The threshold volume level for detecting a Trigger is a certain percentage of the average volume level in the past 30 seconds.  Therefore, if the recent volume level is very loud then this threshold will become higher so that if you play a note quietly immediately after playing loudly the volume rise due to playing the quiet note may not be detected as a trigger. Likewise, if the sound from the microphone is quiet for some time the threshold for detecting a trigger will drift lower so that even the slightest sound may produce a trigger.  This could be important in measuring inharmonicity, for example.  Suppose you take some time placing mutes and getting ready to measure a note.  Then you tap the measure button which causes TuneLab to wait for the next trigger to begin sampling inharmonicity.  But if the trigger detection has become too sensitive due to all the preceding silence, it might detect a trigger too soon and start listening for inharmonicity even before you play the note because of some small random noise.  If there has been some period of silence, play the note several times before tapping the measure button.  Then when you do tap the measure button the trigger detection will not start the measurement prematurely.
