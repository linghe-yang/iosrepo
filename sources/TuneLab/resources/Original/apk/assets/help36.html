Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How can I change from A-440 to A-435 or some other pitch standard?

Tuning to a non-standard pitch is accomplished by adjusting the offset, which can be done by swiping left or right through the Phase Display.  An offset affects all the notes of the scale equally.  The first time you do it a message pops up to confirm that's what you want to do.  To see what frequency you get for A4, select the note A4 and then adjust the offset until the frequency displayed on the screen is what you want.

2. Why is there an offset displayed in the upper right corner of the screen?

The offset is displayed whenever it is not zero.  If you did not intentionally adjust it, then it might have come from a tuning file you loaded, you may have accidentally swiped through the Phase Display, or you may have activated the Locking mode by tapping on the Lock icon.  All these things can potentially result in an offset.  To zero the offset, tap on the offset and select "Reset to Zero."

3. Why did the Phase Display turn yellow?

The Phase Display turns yellow to indicate that it is currently sensitive to adjusting the offset by swiping.  The Phase Display becomes sensitive to this when you confirm in a prompt that you want to adjust the offset, and it remains sensitive until you stop adjusting the offset for at least 45 seconds.  Then it turns its normal colors and is not sensitive to adjusting the offset.

4. Why is the Partial Number changing and flashing?

The Partial Number for the current note flashes briefly when you change it manually or when it is changed automatically by auto partial adjustment.  See Auto Partials in Settings.

5. How can I change which partials is used to tune a note?

Swipe through the Spectrum Display all the way from the left to the right or the right to the left to adjust the current partial number up or down until you get the partial you want.  You should also check the setting of the Persistent Partials switch in Settings if you want your partial change to remain with the tuning file.  You can also change the whole table of partials from Edit Table of Partials in Settings.

6. Where can I find my tuning files outside of TuneLab?

The tuning files you create are stored in a private area of file storage that is only accessible to the TuneLab program.  For outside access you can use TuneLab to upload all your tuning files to a Dropbox cloud storage where you can do whatever you want with them. See the Files menu for information on Dropbox.  You can also export tuning files to a folder in external memory memory from the About this Version page in Settings.  This memory is accessible through a USB connection to a desktop computer.

7. Do I need to do a calibration?

In most cases, no. The built-in pitch reference in most Android devices is a quartz crystal oscillator carefully trimmed at the factory to within 0.1 cents.  But if you suspect your Android device is not registering known frequencies accurately you can calibrate to an external reference pitch or you can calibrate to Internet Time Servers.
